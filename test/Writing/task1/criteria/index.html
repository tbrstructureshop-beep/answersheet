The Four Assessment Criteria Explained

Why Does IELTS Use This 4-Pillar Analytical Matrix?

In international academic and professional spheres, writing competence is not a singular impression; it requires balancing factual precision, logical architecture, lexical versatility, and syntactic control. By separating evaluation into four independent dimensions, IELTS eliminates examiner subjectivity and ensures that a candidate who writes beautifully but misinterprets data is evaluated accurately, while one with sharp analytical insights but limited vocabulary receives precise, diagnostically grounded band scores.

Task Achievement TA
Weight: 25% of Total Task 1 Score
See Band 1–9 TA Benchmark
Official Cambridge Definition

Evaluates how fully, accurately, and relevantly the candidate fulfills the instructions outlined in the rubric: providing an objective summary, choosing key features, supporting comparisons with data, and meeting the 150-word minimum requirement without personal bias.

Band Ceiling Impact

Without a clear Overview, your Task Achievement score is capped at Band 5, regardless of how advanced your grammar or vocabulary is. Missing major trends or reporting inaccurate figures caps Task Achievement at Band 6.

Why is Task Achievement evaluated as an independent criterion?
In universities and corporate workplaces, professionals must extract factual insights from visual data (financial forecasts, lab charts, census tables) without distorting facts or projecting subjective assumptions. Task Achievement measures your analytical literacy: whether you can distinguish between a critical overarching macro-trend and negligible micro-fluctuations, and communicate essential business intelligence accurately.
Clear Overview
A dedicated summary identifying the most prominent trajectories, peaks, troughs, or disparities.
Key Feature Selection
Focusing on significant turning points, rapid surges, or convergence rather than cataloging every minor data point.
Data Accuracy & Integrity
Reporting exact figures from the stimulus without misreading dates, axes, or percentage values.
Strict Objectivity
Total absence of personal opinions, advice, or external causal speculations not depicted in the chart.
Coherence & Cohesion CC
Weight: 25% of Total Task 1 Score
See Band 1–9 CC Benchmark
Official Cambridge Definition

Concerns the clarity, structural organization, and narrative progression of the report. Assesses how smoothly ideas flow between sentences and paragraphs through logical sequencing, accurate cohesive devices, and unambiguous referencing.

Band Ceiling Impact

Relying on a mechanical list of transition signals (Firstly, Secondly, Then, After that) caps CC at Band 5. Band 7 requires clear paragraph-level central topics and flexible referencing (the former, this disparity). At Band 9, cohesion attracts no attention whatsoever.

Why is Coherence & Cohesion evaluated as an independent criterion?
A piece of writing can possess accurate facts and complex words, but if it is jumbled or requires the reader to re-read sentences to discern relationships, it fails as academic communication. CC measures your ability to guide the reader through complex data smoothly, establishing clear spatial, comparative, and chronological links between diverse facts.
Macro-Progression
Organizing data into structured paragraphs (e.g., Intro $\to$ Overview $\to$ High Performers $\to$ Low Performers).
Micro-Cohesion
Using discourse markers (conversely, while, during the subsequent epoch) to connect clauses naturally.
Referencing & Substitution
Replacing repeated country names or statistics with pronouns and noun phrases (the market leader, that of Country C).
Logical Categorization
Avoiding random jumps back and forth across different categories or unrelated calendar years.
Lexical Resource LR
Weight: 25% of Total Task 1 Score
See Band 1–9 LR Benchmark
Official Cambridge Definition

Evaluates the range, precision, register, and appropriateness of the vocabulary used. Considers your capability to paraphrase the prompt, utilize less-common academic collocations, deploy accurate trend descriptors, and maintain clean orthography (spelling and word formation).

Band Ceiling Impact

Copying word-for-word from the question prompt directly limits your score. Repetitive basic verbs (was, went up, had) anchor LR to Band 5. Band 7 requires natural, less-common collocations (penetration rate, narrowing the gap), while Band 9 demonstrates sophisticated, idiomatic control.

Why is Lexical Resource evaluated as an independent criterion?
Statistical reporting demands an exact vocabulary. Conflating "percentage" with "percentage points", or confusing "plateaued" with "fluctuated", alters the factual meaning of a dataset. Lexical Resource measures whether you have the verbal precision to convey nuanced degrees of change (e.g., marginal uptick vs. formidable surge) without resorting to repetitive or informal phrasing.
Analytical Paraphrasing
Rephrasing prompt items (e.g., translating households with internet access into domestic connectivity rates).
Trend Collocations
Pairing verbs and adverbs with mathematical accuracy (rose precipitously, climbed incrementally, crested at).
Comparative Multipliers
Skillfully deploying proportional vocabulary (twofold, eight times higher, a threefold multiplier).
Accurate Word Formation
Flawless shifts across parts of speech (significant $\to$ significantly; expand $\to$ expansion) with zero spelling errors.
Grammatical Range & Accuracy GRA
Weight: 25% of Total Task 1 Score
See Band 1–9 GRA Benchmark
Official Cambridge Definition

Evaluates both the diversity of grammatical structures deployed and the precision of their technical execution. Measures the ratio of error-free sentences, structural complexity (subordination, passives, participles), tense consistency, and punctuation control.

Band Ceiling Impact

Writing only simple S-V-O sentences limits you to Band 5 even if there are zero errors. Band 7 explicitly demands "frequent error-free sentences" with a variety of complex structures. Band 8 requires that the "majority of sentences are error-free".

Why is Grammatical Range & Accuracy evaluated as an independent criterion?
Complex datasets contain multi-layered relationships (e.g., one nation accelerated while another lagged, despite initial parity). Simple grammar cannot express these simultaneous conditions efficiently. GRA measures whether you possess the syntactic dexterity to compress multi-dimensional comparative information into clear, grammatically cohesive sentences without causing reader strain.
Syntactic Variety
Balancing compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences with relative and adverbial clauses.
Non-Finite Participles
Condensing data cleanly using present and past participle phrases (...rising to 75%, followed by...).
Tense & Voice Consistency
Maintaining appropriate past tense for concluded historical periods and utilizing academic passive constructions.
Punctuation Precision
Flawless placement of commas in compound sentences, parenthetical em-dashes, and semicolons.
